# Packages: the working offers

Status: **Sean's working set of offers since September 29, 2026, reworked for his floors on September 30, 2026.** The senior CTO agency designed them; Sean works with them as they stand and can change any share, name or amount. The site's pricing lines on the homepage, the service pages and the contact page follow them. The prices page stays the cornerstone, and Sean owns it; every figure here is a share of its two published figures, $6,000 a month and $120,000 a year.

**The floors (Sean's direction, pass 8).** The team takes no ongoing client below the $6,000 monthly engagement, and a one-time job starts at a third share ($2,000), a floor set by demand. Small edits aren't offered: the team hasn't the entry-level webmasters for quick jobs, so recruiting comes first, and owner pages state the floors without mentioning small edits. The senior CTO reworked the ladder below to fit.

## The model: one rate, bought in shares

- **The unit is the monthly engagement at $6,000:** a retainer that grants access to the team and the results its written scope names, each month. Fees pay for results, never time: no hourly or daily rates, and a share is never a count of hours or days.
- **A share** is a defined portion of that engagement at the same rate: a third, a half or a full share, and larger jobs in whole shares. Bought once, a share is a flat fee for a defined job. The only ongoing offers are the monthly and annual engagements; ongoing care, local search, ads and conversion work held month to month are part of the monthly engagement.
- **The annual engagement at $120,000 is twenty months of work carried across twelve** (20 × $6,000): the monthly engagement's own scope every month (12 × $6,000 = $72,000), four months of technology planning ($24,000) and four months of an ADA Title II / WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility program ($24,000). It is a larger scope at the same rate, never 12 × the month and never a discount.
- **Fractional CTO work stays minimal here.** The site mentions it lightly and points it to the sister practice at sdin.dev; the annual engagement's technology planning is the part that stays with Sean Dinwiddie's Webmastery.
- Fees rise in exact proportion to the work, so more work costs more, nothing is discounted, and a kiosk and a county office pay the same rate.
- "Share" is a share of an engagement's work, never ownership, equity or an investment. Shares name the size of the job, never the worth of the client. The site never breaks a share into days, weeks or hours.

1. **The Third Share ($2,000):** one job with a known end, and the smallest the team takes. One page designed, built and handed over (a food truck's menu, an event page, a clinic's booking page); one automation, built, connected and handed over; local search set up for one location (the Business Profile in the owner's name, listings matched on Google, Apple Maps, Bing, Yelp and Facebook, and structured data to match); one conversion leak found, fixed and measured; one site's technical search fix list, worked through; one change to a site after handover; a team training session with written notes; one piece of artwork in the site's look; **the automation audit** (the work watched where it happens, and a written list of what to automate, what to leave, roughly what each saves and the share each would take); **a consultation** (written notes: what to build, change or leave, and the shares each part would take). The audit and the consultation are never credited toward a build; a defined job needs neither, because its written scope is the offer. Includes the written scope, the work, a check that it works and a short note. A bigger job found on the way is scoped as its own share and approved first.
2. **The Half Share ($3,000):** an accessibility review against WCAG 2.1 AA for the templates the scope names (findings in priority order, a remediation plan priced in shares, a draft accessibility statement), the entry point for a public office; a refresh of an existing site. Excludes remediation, document fixes and legal advice.
3. **The Full Share ($6,000), and whole shares:** a website designed, built and handed over (hours, menu or prices, map, tap-to-call, a link to the booking tool the owner already uses, structured data, listings); the written scope lists the pages, what each one does and who supplies the words and photos; remediation of the templates a review names; the first share of a custom app, ending in screens you can click through and a written fee for the rest. Two shares ($12,000) for a site that takes orders, bookings or payments itself, or covers several locations; custom builds in three to twelve shares ($18,000 to $72,000), each approved before it starts.
4. **The Monthly Engagement ($6,000 a month):** a site and systems held every month: ongoing care (hosting managed in the owner's own account, certificates, updates, backups, the uptime watch, security patches, and a webmaster who knows the site), local search, ads at a flat fee (never a percentage of spend; the owner pays the platform directly under a written ceiling), conversion work, automation upkeep, and the monthly work list and planning call, with a written monthly note. New builds beyond the work list are scoped as shares; ad spend and third-party fees are outside it; technology planning and the accessibility program belong to the year. It never pays the owner's bills: hosting, the domain and tools are billed to the owner by each provider.
5. **The Annual Engagement ($120,000 a year, billed monthly):** the monthly engagement every month, plus technology planning (an annual technology plan, vendor and contract review, procurement support, a security review; fractional CTO leadership beyond it goes through sdin.dev) and an accessibility program (a review of sites, apps and key documents, remediation of the office's own web content within scope, an Accessibility Conformance Report, the statement and reporting process, staff training, quarterly conformance notes).

**After handover.** The site runs in the owner's own accounts, set to update itself where the stack allows (WordPress core, plugins and theme on auto-update), and the owner is shown how to run it. Under the monthly engagement, changes go on the month's written work list; outside it, a change after handover is a one-time job from a third share.

**Public offices.** An office starts with an accessibility review at a half share ($3,000), then remediation in whole shares, each scoped after the review, or holds the annual engagement, which carries the whole program.

**Retired in pass 8** (the site never mentions them): the tenth share ($600) and quarter share ($1,500), below the one-time floor; the Care Contract ($300 and $600 a month), the Standing Shares ($1,500 and $3,000 a month) and the Year Share ($24,000 a year), ongoing below the monthly engagement; and shared engagements, where each owner would pay less than $6,000 a month.

The community's memberships (General Access at $1,000 a month and the Inner Circle at $4,000 a month) sit beside the ladder, not on it, and appear only on Training and the community pages. They are for webmasters who want more mentorship and camaraderie, which comes much later (Sean, pass 8); joining the team never depends on them.

## Decisions and guardrails

Sean works with the model as it stands: one rate sold in shares, the year as twenty months of work, and the denominations (third, half, full and whole shares; the monthly and annual engagements). Decisions still his:

- Names: "the monthly engagement" and "the annual engagement", or "the Month" and "the Year".
- Delivery: shares are not time; each scope names its own delivery date.
- **Settled: retainer terms** (Sean's direction, September 30, 2026; `docs/terms.md`): month to month, billed monthly in advance, 30 days' notice from either side; nothing rolls over; the annual engagement is billed monthly ($10,000) and can be ended for convenience. On the site since pass 12.
- Ownership (recommended: the owner holds the domain, hosting and accounts; code and content are the owner's once paid; the webmaster's pre-existing tools are licensed, not transferred).
- Third-party costs (recommended: billed to the owner directly by each provider, so every fee stays a share).
- Payment schedules and travel between Klamath Falls and Redding.
- **Settled: no free work** (Sean's direction, pass 8). The team provides no free work, audits and consulting included: the automation audit and a consultation are each a third share ($2,000) with written notes, never credited toward the build. The site offers no free call, conversation or consultation; the way in is an inquiry that names the job.
- **Settled: paying for a one-time job** (senior CTO, pass 10): a third share in full at signing, against the scope's result and delivery date; a half share and up half at signing and half on acceptance (`docs/terms.md`).
- **Settled: the floors** (Sean, pass 8): a third share ($2,000) is the smallest job the team takes, set by demand, and it buys the same standard as the largest job. Owners change hours, prices, photos and specials themselves after handover, so the floor applies only to changes that need a webmaster, and several changes are never bundled into one share.
- Commission: the house keeps 30% and the webmaster keeps 70%, inside the published fee. Sean's direction (`docs/terms.md`): a flat 70/30 on everything run through the agency, referred or brought in, one-time or held each month; counsel puts it in legal form. Commission is an incentive, so the protection is the change rule: the client decides every addition, and every out-of-scope quote needs the client's approval.
- Procurement: registration (OregonBuys, Cal eProcure), insurance, W-9 and licenses. Oregon's small procurement goes up to $25,000 (ORS 279B.065), so a half-share accessibility review fits under it; $25,000 to $250,000 needs three quotes (ORS 279B.070), where the $120,000 year falls. Splitting a procurement to stay under a threshold is prohibited, so the review is its own deliverable and is never pitched as a way under a threshold.

Legal cautions:

- Accessibility work is technical conformance work against WCAG 2.1 AA, reported as status on a stated date. Never "ADA compliant", "certified", "guaranteed", "lawsuit-proof" or "100%". The office's own counsel decides legal questions. No accessibility overlays (the FTC's April 2025 order against accessiBe). An Accessibility Conformance Report is a self-report, not a certification.
- No ranking guarantees; reviews never bought or rewarded; ads never paid by percentage of spend. The ADA Title II dates are the only urgency, stated plainly.
