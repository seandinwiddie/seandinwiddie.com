## Council 1: the webmaster deal
## Where the Council Agrees

All five, independently:
- **Joining is free:** no fee, membership, course or purchase. "Team Staff Discounts" comes off recruit-facing pages. The community stays, but joining never depends on it. With no required payment, the FTC Business Opportunity Rule doesn't come into play.
- **Webmasters keep every outside client and all outside work.**
- **The agency invoices and collects.** Webmasters are paid within seven days of the client's payment.
- **Webmasters bring their own** registration, insurance, tools and licences, and set their own hours.
- **The builder keeps the Care Contract.** The owner can switch anytime.
- **Portfolio credit** under the webmaster's own name, "for Sean Dinwiddie's Webmastery."
- **Free fixes cover defects in the webmaster's own work.** Anything new is quoted.
- **Require one thing only:** a review of results before launch.

Four of the five also back one flat 70/30 split and a rewrite of the old pay page.

### Where the Council Clashes

- **The split.** The Contrarian wants 85/15 on work the webmaster finds, because 30% buys little from a name with four reviews. The others want one number, since a sliding split turns into a negotiation. The First Principles Thinker answers the worry: nobody has to run their own client through the agency. The flat split wins.
- **Non-solicit.** The Expansionist and the Outsider want 12 months on referred clients. The others want none, because California voids most non-solicits (Edwards v. Arthur Andersen) and the clause reads as control. The owner chooses their webmaster, and that settles it: no non-solicit.
- **The ladder.** Three advisors set rungs by job size. The Contrarian sets them by split. The First Principles Thinker sets them by how much review the work needs. All five peer reviews backed the last. Job-size rungs send the junior to the food truck, and a split that changes by rung is pay by level.
- **The old pay page.** The Executor wants an archive line plus noindex today. The rest say a disclaimer over "-$7,200" is still an MLM exhibit to anyone who clicks through. Rewrite it.

### Blind Spots the Council Caught

- **Who owns the code.** A contractor's work isn't work-for-hire by default. "No lock-in" needs a written assignment from webmaster to agency to owner, with the domain, hosting and logins in the owner's name.
- **Exit terms:** who takes over Care Contracts and credentials when a webmaster leaves or is removed.
- **Liability:** the agency signs the client contract, so it needs professional liability cover and an indemnity from each webmaster.
- **Government procurement** may bar subcontracting or set insurance minimums. That decides whether ADA Title II work can be team work.
- **The legal core.** §2783 exempts insurance agents, not web developers, so the insurance-agency comparison fails in California. §2777's referral-agency exemption may cover web design, but it expects the webmaster to set their own rates with nothing deducted. No proposal meets §2776's criterion about serving the contracting business's customers.
- **Sean's conflict:** he hands out referrals and also competes for them.

### The Recommendation

Counsel reads the whole term sheet either way. Each line is marked with what it needs before it can close.

1. **Split:** 70/30 on everything run through the agency (referred, self-sourced, builds, Care, Standing), with what the 30% buys listed beside it: the name, the contract, billing, collection and review. *Needs counsel* (one published rate and a deducted 30% against §2776/§2777).
2. **Joining:** free, stated in one sentence. "Team Staff Discounts" comes off, and the community stays. *Needs only Sean's approval.*
3. **Client:** the owner owns the site. The agency holds the contracts run through it, and webmasters keep all outside clients. No non-solicit, only this rule: "work under an agency contract is billed through the agency while that contract runs." *Needs counsel.*
4. **Business side:** whoever finds the lead takes the first call, and the webmaster drafts the scope on the agency template. The agency confirms the fee, bills before work starts, collects and chases late payments. The webmaster is paid within seven days of collection and nothing is ever clawed back. Referrals are handed out by a written rule. *Needs only Sean's approval.*
5. **Capacity:** the webmaster brings their own registration, insurance, tools, licences and hours. The agency supplies only the name, templates and billing. *Needs counsel* (insurance minimums, the agency's own cover).
6. **Recurring:** the builder keeps the Care Contract at 70/30 for as long as the owner wants them, and the owner picks any successor. *Needs only Sean's approval.*
7. **Portfolio:** yes, credited "[name] for Sean Dinwiddie's Webmastery." *Needs only Sean's approval.*
8. **Ladder:** the split stays flat and any rung takes any job. A junior's launch needs a senior's review, a mid's needs any peer's, and seniors review others' work. Entry and promotion rest on delivered, reviewed results. *Needs only Sean's approval.*
9. **Making things right:** webmasters fix defects in their own work free, without asking. Anything else is quoted, because free extra work is a discount. *Needs only Sean's approval.*
10. **Shared learning:** require only a second webmaster's review of results before launch. Notes, calls and pairing stay optional. *Needs only Sean's approval.*
11. **Old pay page:** rewrite the pay section of `/community/staff/` and both sibling pages, removing the community earnings, negative percentages, team-size payouts and "team staff." The pages and the community stay, and the new terms go on after counsel reviews them. *Needs only Sean's approval.*

IP assignment, exit terms, liability and government subcontracting also go on the term sheet. *Needs counsel.*

Every line passes one test: the 30% buys services, never access. The recruiting copy says clients are referred when the agency has them.

### The One Thing to Do First

Sean approves this term sheet and sends it to counsel with one question: can a California webmaster serve the agency's clients at the one published rate, with 30% deducted, and still pass the ABC test, §2776 or §2777? If not, what structure would pass? Every recruiting line waits on that answer.


## Council 2: the owner deal
## Where the Council Agrees

- **The test for every term: can Sean keep it alone, this week?** Four of the five advisors built their answers on this test. The site shows one webmaster, so any promise that needs a second person is an invented specific.
- **Care is optional** (all five). Requiring Care after a "no lock-in" handover is lock-in with a monthly fee.
- **Ownership, with two additions:** the accounts are in the owner's name from day one, and a webmaster's own tools come with a permanent license.
- **Third-party costs billed directly, with no markups or referral fees.** This is the plainest answer to the fear of "a commission webmaster who upsells me."
- **The free call ends with a written scope and fee** within a stated number of days. The free part stops at diagnosis.
- **Only reply times Sean can keep alone; a one-sentence confidentiality promise; competitors are served; AI use is stated plainly.**
- **Every scope names a delivery date.** From a quarter share up, half is paid at signing and half on delivery. Retainers are billed in advance.
- **"The monthly engagement" and "the annual engagement"** (four of five). "The Year" is too easily confused with the Year Share.

### Where the Council Clashes

- **The $600 tenth.** The Contrarian, Outsider and Executor take it up front: owners are used to deposits, and it means no unpaid work. The First Principles Thinker and the Expansionist take it on delivery, so no owner pays a stranger first. The real question is who carries the risk of the first job.
- **A departing webmaster's clients.** Most keep the engagement with the agency. The First Principles Thinker lets the owner follow the webmaster. Keeping it protects the name on the door. Letting the owner follow is a purer form of no lock-in, and it fits the "own clientele" criterion in §2776.
- **Competitors.** The First Principles Thinker would serve both and tell each; the Contrarian would never run search for both. Only one business can hold the top spot in local search, which settles it.
- **Publish now or after a job.** The Expansionist's Owner's Charter makes the terms the proof the site lacks. The Executor wants a real job to run on them first. The Expansionist has the right idea; the Executor has the right timing.
- **Smaller splits:** 30 or 60 days' notice on the annual engagement, and whether to promise a same-day reply when a site that takes orders is down.

### Blind Spots the Council Caught

- **The owner guarantees can undo the webmasters' contractor status** (all five reviews). Reply times set by the agency, assigned cover, switching webmasters and required runbooks look like direction and control under ORS 670.600. Webmasters serving the agency's customers at the agency's rate also cuts against §2776. Counsel should review the owner terms and the subcontractor agreement together.
- **When a job is wrong:** none of the five set acceptance terms, a rework window, refunds, a dispute process, a liability cap or the insurance county buyers check first.
- **No one required removing a departing webmaster's access** to owner accounts.
- **Every reviewer ranked the Contrarian first, for three structural points.** The subcontractor must assign the work to the agency, or the ownership promise is hollow. The webmaster's 70% comes out of the final payment. And a webmaster earns 70% of any scope they write, so every scope should lead with the smallest job that solves the problem.

### The Recommendation

1. **Continuity:** the owner signs with Sean Dinwiddie's Webmastery, and Sean covers. A site record lives in the owner's account. A departing webmaster's access is removed the same day, the terms continue unchanged, and the owner can leave on notice. Promise a change of webmaster only once a second webmaster is named. *Needs counsel or a fact only Sean has.*
2. **Care:** optional. A repair tenth is one named fix at $600; anything larger is quoted before it starts. *Needs only Sean's approval.*
3. **Retainers:** no minimum first term. Every engagement, annual ones included, is billed monthly in advance, with 30 days' notice and termination for convenience for everyone. Nothing rolls over, but any undelivered scope is finished the next month. *Needs counsel.*
4. **One-time shares:** a tenth is paid at signing. A quarter and up is half at signing and half on acceptance. Government pays on its own invoice terms. *Needs only Sean's approval.*
5. **Ownership:** approve. The accounts are in the owner's name, subcontractors assign their work to the agency, and pre-existing tools get a permanent license so the site keeps working without that webmaster. *Needs counsel.*
6. **Third-party costs:** billed directly by each provider. The scope lists each provider and a typical cost. No markups or referral fees. *Needs a fact only Sean has.*
7. **First call:** it ends with a written scope and fee, or a written no, within two business days. The free part covers diagnosis and an audit given out loud on the call. Logging in, changing anything or writing up the audit is paid work. *Needs a fact only Sean has.*
8. **Hours:** the hours Sean actually keeps, in Pacific time, with a reply by the next business day and no same-day promise. *Needs a fact only Sean has.*
9. **Confidentiality:** one sentence on the site and in every scope. Serve competitors and tell an owner on request, but never run local search for two direct competitors in the same market. *Needs counsel.*
10. **AI:** one line saying webmasters use AI tools, a named webmaster reviews and answers for every piece of work, and no customer data or passwords go into those tools. *Needs a fact only Sean has.*
11. **Names:** "the monthly engagement" and "the annual engagement." *Needs only Sean's approval.*
12. **Delivery dates:** every scope names a delivery date and a date for the owner's inputs, and delivery moves back a day for each day the inputs are late. Each scope opens like a receipt: the job, the fee, the date and the webmaster. *Needs only Sean's approval.*

### The One Thing to Do First

Write a one-page scope-and-fee template using these twelve clauses. Send it to counsel together with the subcontractor agreement, so contractor status, the assignment of work, acceptance and warranty all get one review before any term reaches the site.


## Council 3: the site and brand
## Where the Council Agrees

- **The buttons pass before the site sells accessibility.** Four of five advisors find the same fix: set button labels as WCAG "large text" (18.66px bold or 24px). Large text only needs 3:1, so 3.9:1 passes and every color stays.
- **The prices page leads with prices.** The ladder goes at the top in dollars. "120k/annu", the "unique value proposition" heading, the mission and the video go below it or come out.
- **The community runs under Sean Dinwiddie's Webmastery,** without the "Software Firm/Practice/Consults" byline or the "agency technical archive" banner. Nothing is removed.
- **Legal starts by removing what's false,** beginning with the SMS terms for a form that doesn't exist.
- **Thin pages.** Retire `/store/` and the empty "Cargo cult programming" post. Source the `/tools/` quotes or cut them. Cut "use WordPress" and make Training the handover guides.
- **The root problem is too many names.** Several names and one visible person read as a solo operator dressed as an agency.

### Where the Council Clashes

- **Pause the offer or keep selling.** The Contrarian and the Outsider pause; the Expansionist and the Executor fix fast. The fix is one line of CSS, so shipping it first ends the argument.
- **Size or color.** The Expansionist darkens the blue; the Executor does so only if Sean counts label size as "fonts." That question decides decision 1.
- **Growth or truth.** The Expansionist adds Medford, promises Redding visits, keeps the 🧙 and pitches the club as ongoing compliance. The rest publish only what's true today. Each expansion is a claim the site can't back yet.
- **Procurement order.** The First Principles Thinker registers last, since a registration with nothing behind it wins nothing. The Executor starts on day one, because registration and insurance take weeks. Start the slow items now; hold the page.
- **Who the "administrators" are:** owners after handover, everyone local, or webmasters, which is the FTC risk.

### Blind Spots the Council Caught

- **Which way the pricing fix runs.** No one asked whether Sean approved the ladder, and the repo's docs list it as awaiting his approval.
- **Legal entity.** A W-9 and registrations need a legal name and a taxpayer ID. Without an assumed business name filing, the W-9 adds another name.
- **Redirects.** Retired pages need them, or a site that sells local search serves 404s.
- **Subcontractors on public work.** This means flow-down clauses, insurance covering their work, public-records data terms and a confidentiality clause in the fee agreement.
- **Capacity.** No one asked how many remediation jobs can be delivered by April 2027.
- **Moving dates.** DOJ has moved the Title II dates once already; the page cites the rule.
- Only the Contrarian caught the FTC Business Opportunity Rule risk. The Expansionist's "only published rate card of any agency in Jefferson State" is invented.

### The Recommendation

1. **Accessibility.** Set button labels as large text and pause the offer until that ships. Add a contrast check to `npm run build`. Audit the site against WCAG 2.1 AA before pitching any public office, and publish a conformance statement covering only what the audit verifies. If Sean counts size as a font change, this bends that rule. The fallback, one step darker blue, bends the color rule and is the only exception worth making. *Needs only Sean's approval.*
2. **Prices page.** Sean confirms the ladder is his, then puts it at the top in dollars. If it isn't his, the other pages go back to his two figures. *Needs only Sean's approval.*
3. **Community.** One sentence defines administrators as the owners and office staff who run their site after handover. It runs under the brand name, without the banner or the byline. A written line says membership is never a condition of joining the team or of any engagement, and it is never sold as compliance. *Needs only Sean's approval; counsel only if webmasters become members.*
4. **Legal.** Take the SMS terms down now. Until counsel rewrites the privacy policy, no subcontractor handles client data, so today's policy stays true. One email to counsel covers governing law, the privacy policy and the confidentiality clause. *Needs counsel.*
5. **Thin pages.** Retire `/store/` and the post, with redirects. Cut the unsourced `/tools/` quotes and retire the page if nothing is left. Rewrite Training as the handover guides, without the WordPress line. *Needs only Sean's approval.*
6. **Service area.** Cut Medford unless Sean serves it now; if he does, name it everywhere. *Needs a fact only Sean has.*
7. **Redding.** Offer shop meetings on scheduled days, or video, only if Sean commits to the drive. *Needs a fact only Sean has.*
8. **🧙.** Retire it, which touches no color or font. If Sean keeps it, mark it `aria-hidden` so screen readers stop saying "mage." *Needs only Sean's approval.*
9. **Public offices.** Confirm the legal name and file the assumed business name, then:
   - fill out the W-9
   - get insurance that covers subcontractors
   - register on OregonBuys, then Cal eProcure
   - build the page last, once a clerk could hire the day they read it

   The page cites the DOJ rule, is sized to real capacity, and gives sdin.dev one line at most. *Needs counsel, plus a fact only Sean has (capacity).*

### The One Thing to Do First

Get Sean's ruling that label size isn't a "font" change. Then ship button labels at 18.66px bold, check them against 3:1 and push after `npm run build`. The offer and the public-office page wait on it.
