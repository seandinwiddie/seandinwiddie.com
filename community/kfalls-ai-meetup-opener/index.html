KFalls AI Meetup (evening, business & student)

The shopkeeper who remembers you A first look at Forboc AI

She speaks in her own words and keeps to the game’s rules

Good evening, everyone, and welcome. Before tonight’s two longer talks, we’ve brought a little appetizer, about five minutes long. It’s for anyone who has ever wondered how the characters in a video game could feel a bit more alive. If you’ve never made a game, you’re in just the right seat, and we’ll keep the words plain. And if you run a business, there’s one idea in here that travels well. We’ll point it out.

The picture is a tiny painted knight, riding through a courtyard full of lanterns. It’s a world that looks lived in. Could the people in a world like that feel lived in, too? Let’s meet one of them.

Picture a shopkeeper

Last time, you bought her old key. Today, she remembers.

You ask for a legendary sword. She can’t sell what she hasn’t got.

And her answer isn’t a line anyone wrote ahead of time.

Pieced together from the docs’ own examples: the merchant, the old key and the missing sword

Let’s picture her together: a little shop in a game, and behind the counter, a cautious merchant guarding rare stock. Game makers call a character like her an NPC, a non-player character. That just means someone in the game who’s played by the computer, not by a person.

In many games, a shopkeeper says the same few lines forever. This one is different in three small ways. She remembers what you did. She can’t sell you what she hasn’t got. And her words aren’t picked from a list someone wrote; they’re written fresh, right then.

We didn’t make her up, either. Every piece of her comes from the examples in Forboc AI’s docs. All we did was stand them behind one counter.

What Forboc AI is

Forboc AI gives a game’s characters a memory and a voice of their own, inside the game’s rules.

A model writes what she says, but it only proposes. In Forboc AI’s words, every action is “a proposal your game is free to refuse.”

Sources: the docs’ welcome page and reference, and Forboc.ai

So where does she come from? Forboc AI, which calls itself the NPC language model company. A language model is a program that has read a great deal of writing and learned to write back. Let’s walk through one turn on the drawing, top to bottom.

You ask her for a legendary sword. First, she looks through what she remembers: oh yes, you bought the old key. Next, a small model writes what she might say and do. Say it gets carried away and offers you a sword. Last, the game’s rules check that offer. She hasn’t got one, and in the docs’ sample, the answer is a plain no, with the reason.

For the engineers, one picture from the docs: Forboc AI’s service is the mind, which directs each step and checks the result, and the kit inside your game is the body, which writes the words. And for those of you who run a business and wonder who holds the records: Forboc AI says her memories go to storage the game’s own studio owns and can read without them.

And here’s the idea we promised: the model proposes, and the rules decide. It’s a good one to keep in your pocket tonight.

Who it’s for

  • Unreal Engine 5.7: a plugin, in the docs today
  • The web, or an engine of your own: a kit for those, too
  • Writers and designers: a studio in the browser, no code needed
  • Unity and Godot: the docs list both as in development

Sources: the docs’ welcome page, the studio’s page and the Unity and Godot pages

So who’s it for? Anyone making a game whose characters ought to feel a little more alive. If you build in Unreal Engine, there’s a plugin for version 5.7, written up in the docs today. If your game runs on the web, or on an engine of your own, there’s a kit for that too.

If you’d rather write characters than code, the docs describe a studio in your web browser. You design a character, try talking with it and export it, all without writing a line of code.

Unity and Godot friends, we see you. The docs list both as in development, and studios that would like early access are invited to write in. And a gentle note for the students: as of the docs, the version number is still under one. It’s young software, and that’s part of the fun.

So that’s Forboc AI in one breath: a shopkeeper who remembers you, keeps to the game’s rules and answers in her own words.

And here’s where we leave you, right at the door, on purpose. Picture asking her the very same thing tomorrow. Will she give you the same answer? We won’t tell you. One clue: she keeps notes on what happens, each marked by how much it matters. The rest is in the docs, and it makes a lovely puzzle for tonight, with a phone in one hand and a snack in the other. Start at docs.forboc.ai, pick your engine and go find the cautious merchant.

One more little door, for the puzzle lovers: near the top of a few pages there’s a line of runes. We’ll let you work out what they spell. Now, let’s settle in for the evening’s two longer talks.

The cautious merchant, the note about the old key and the sample answer about the legendary sword are all in Forboc AI’s docs, at docs.forboc.ai. They open on a welcome page that asks you to pick your engine.
