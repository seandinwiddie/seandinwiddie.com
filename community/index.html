Community: User stories, BDD & FRP

Work with Sean

Agency lessons. How the Sean Dinwiddie’s Webmastery team builds custom software: user stories, behavior-driven development and functional programming, and on to agents and small language models. Every level starts with the Introduction, and the course outline maps every module.

Features are scoped from user stories, each story’s acceptance criteria become Gherkin scenarios, and the scenarios drive the RTK Query endpoints, Redux Toolkit slices and minimal React views, with a Haskell (Servant) API behind them. The API’s type describes itself as an OpenAPI document, and RTK Query’s generator turns that document into the client’s endpoints, so server and client share one contract. One feature, scope to launch carries one feature from its written scope to the review before launch.

The stack follows the project: many brochure sites are easiest for owners to run on WordPress, set to update automatically, while custom apps and features that hold state follow the chain these lessons teach.

Beside the chain, Module 5 puts an agent to work on a business process, with a person approving what leaves the building, and Module 6 teaches the open protocols an AI system crosses and how the team trains and evaluates small language models.

Sean’s Functional Programming Lectures teach the functional TypeScript and Redux foundation underneath, among them What Is a Function? and Modern Redux Architecture Patterns.

Webmasters at any stage of the craft can work under the Sean Dinwiddie’s Webmastery name, which brings in local clients, and keep 70% of the fee on their work. The team and this community are one membership, earned through the quality of the work, and members meet at meetups such as the KFalls AI Meetup, bring their questions and learn from one another. Joining the team sets out the terms and how to join.
