Joining the team

Call or email
Updated September 30, 2026· Development
Sean Dinwiddie

Joining the Sean Dinwiddie’s Webmastery team asks one thing: quality work. Webmasters join at every stage of the craft, junior included, and joining begins with the work itself, the first job paid like every other: every job has a written scope and is reviewed against it before it goes live, and the name vouches for the result. No fee, course or purchase stands in the way. The review checks results, never how the work was done; on a custom app, its passing scenarios are part of the result.

70% of the fee on your work

You work as a subcontractor from your own practice and keep 70% of the fee on your work. The agency keeps 30%, inside the published fee, never added to the owner’s bill. The 30% pays for the name, the contract, billing and collection, and the review before launch; each job comes with a written subcontract naming your fee and the date it is paid. The clients you bring in stay yours, and so do your outside clients; work under an agency contract is billed through the agency while that contract runs.

The 70% comes from client work

Your 70% comes from the fees on the client work you deliver, never from recruiting, memberships or the community’s growth.

For example:

Say you build and hand over a family shop’s website, a full share of the $6,000 monthly engagement. Your 70% is $4,200, and the agency keeps $1,800. When an owner holds the monthly engagement with you as their webmaster, your 70% is $4,200 each month it runs. The smallest job the team takes is a third share ($2,000), and 70% of it is $1,400.

The ladder

The split is the same on every job run through the agency, one-time or held each month and whoever brought it in, and at every rung of the ladder, from junior to mid to senior: webmasters move up on delivered, reviewed results, and any rung takes any job. What a rung changes is the peer review before Sean’s: a junior’s launch gets a senior’s review, a mid’s gets a mid’s or a senior’s, and seniors review others’ work; then Sean reviews every launch against the written scope.

Your name on your work

You show your work under your own name, credited “for Sean Dinwiddie’s Webmastery”, and look after the sites you build for as long as the owner holds the monthly engagement and wants you. When you are away, the engagement stays with Sean Dinwiddie’s Webmastery and Sean covers the work; the fee follows the work, so a month someone else delivers is paid to them.

What the team brings

Joining the team comes with the Sean Dinwiddie’s Webmastery name, local work, a published fee and a written scope for every job, so the time on each job goes to the craft, the team’s course teaches the practice from the first user story to the API behind it, and a lesson learned on one job does not stop with one webmaster. The stack follows the project: many brochure sites run on WordPress, set to update themselves, while custom apps follow the chain the course teaches, and each written scope names the stack. Webmasters on the team belong to the online country club through the quality of their work, never by buying in; its memberships are separate offers, and joining the team never depends on them.
